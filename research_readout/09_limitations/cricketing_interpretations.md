# Cricketing interpretations

These readings rest on the computed numbers. Each one says which number it rests on. They describe what the model measures, not claims about players beyond the data.

1. **Batting pressure is now mostly the match situation.** The batter high band covers 58.9% of death balls, 86.4% of final balls and 10.9% of powerplay balls. Recent wickets still matter: 75.6% of high-band balls follow one, against 27.5% of all balls. In the high band, strike rate is 132.8 against 142.6 in the zero band, and the striker-out rate 6.99% against 4.88%.
2. **Bowling pressure now leans to the new ball.** 72.1% of pace high-band balls are in the powerplay and 10.5% at the death (M-17). Economy on legal balls from the zero to the high band: pace 8.46 → 9.25, spin 7.26 → 8.68. A death specialist's high-band sample is now smaller than an opening bowler's.
3. **Spin carries the middle overs.** 60.1% of spin high-band balls are in the middle overs and 35.5% in the powerplay.
4. **The batting top 10 by role** is 4/5/1 (top/middle/lower). Lower-order ranked batters have 55.8% of their balls in the high band, against 12.3% for top-order batters: death overs and wickets down are now the main route into the band.
5. **The role mix is a modelling choice.** Top 20 (top/middle/lower): role-only 4/12/4, 50/50 blend 6/10/4, pooled 8/9/3. Before the fixes the same switch moved it from 6/7/7 to 14/6/0. Batters are compared partly within their batting role because the roles are different jobs. An opener faces the new ball with no wickets down and is judged on building an innings; a finisher arrives at the death, often after a collapse, and is judged on scoring fast against set bowlers. A strike rate or average is not comparable across those jobs without some adjustment. Pooling every batter rewards the role whose numbers are easiest to post. A role-only z-score assumes each role is equally deep in talent. Neither is a finding about cricket. The 50/50 blend (ROLE_BLEND = 0.5) is a stated compromise between the two.
6. **Batting ranks rest mostly on** baseline 29.1%, high pressure 27.2%, clutch 21.2%, peers 11.5% (variance shares).
7. **Bowling ranks rest mostly on** high pressure 25.9%, clutch 20.9%, baseline 19.9%, peers 13.5%. Baseline-only and full rankings share 10 of the top 20.
8. **Tail situations no longer distort ball value much.** At 8–9 down, batters average -0.088 runs-equivalent per ball (before -0.421), and bowlers facing them 0.057 (before 0.391).
9. **The 2025 final chase.** PBKS's 7 wickets fell at legal balls 30, 51, 58, 73, 98, 100, 104. Batter pressure peaks at 8.35 on ball 99. 120 of the 120 balls are batter-high (a final at occasion 3.0).
